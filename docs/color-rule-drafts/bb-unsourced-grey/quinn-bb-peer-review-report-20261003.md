# BB lift ROM evidence report (peer-reviewed basis), Oct 3 2026

**RESEARCH ONLY. NOTHING IN THIS REPORT CHANGES THE APP.** No Supabase or live system was touched and nothing was sent to anyone. Files were written on the research computer, and copies were uploaded to Drive (30 Research) plus one Notion page.

Author: Quinn (Kinesiology Research) for Jim Scott / ROMRx LLC. Basis: the four Grok Heavy answers (A lower, B hinge/pull, C push/arms/core, D remaining), every cited paper re-checked independently on PubMed (NCBI E-utilities), Crossref, Europe PMC full text, and public web excerpts. Perplexity is not used as a source anywhere.

## 10-line summary

1. **Papers checked:** 51 distinct citations (50 cited by Heavy, plus 1 I resolved myself: Lorenzetti 2018). **37 VERIFIED, 13 PARTIAL, 1 WRONG.** No DOI or PMID Heavy gave was fabricated. Every cited journal paper I could look up exists and matches title, year and journal (one first author is wrong). Three cited items (McKean 2015, Marchetti 2015, Muyor 2022) were not found in PubMed, so they stay PARTIAL. One citation was dropped (a repository report with no DOI).
2. **Family x joint cells:** the 27 families that carry numbers have 70 family x joint cells in the app. Only **20 of 70 (29%)** have at least one exercise matched to a published paper; **50 have nothing (UNSOURCED).**
3. **Verdicts on those 70 cells:** SUPPORTS 3, CONTRADICTS 3, PARTIAL/NOT COMPARABLE 14, UNSOURCED 50. (Forearm family has 0 numbers: nothing to test. 28th family.)
4. **Share of the 499 app numbers covered by a published source:** **57 of 499 (11.4%)** are matched to a paper at all; only **32 (6.4%)** are matched to a paper that gives a directly comparable angle, and just **24 (4.8%)** to papers I fully VERIFIED. **442 numbers (88.6%) have no published support.**
5. **At number level:** SUPPORTS 15 (9 rest only on fully verified papers; 3 rest on McKean 2015, whose numbers I could not confirm; 3 squat numbers also lean on Swinton 2012, which is PARTIAL), CONTRADICTS 7, PARTIAL/NOT COMPARABLE 35, UNSOURCED 442.
6. **Where the data say the app is too high:** 7 numbers sit well above anything published people reach (hip flexion 100-115 deg asked for Nordic curl, prone leg curl, sumo and Romanian deadlift, good morning and hip thrust; published values are about 9, 30 (a machine set-up angle, not a measured peak), 87, 80, 75 and 83 deg).
7. **Joints with zero published support:** Shoulder ER (86 numbers) and lumbar extension (29) have no paper at all; shoulder flexion is only touched by one seated overhead-press study (McKean 2015, numbers unconfirmed). Lumbar flexion is only sourced as trunk lean, which is not lumbar flexion.
8. **Tier ramp (beginner/intermediate/advanced): NOT supported.** No paper found compares skill or ROM levels for these lifts. Where we can compare, beginner numbers are reached by published people but intermediate and advanced numbers are not (see ramp section).
9. **What Heavy got wrong or overstated:** 1 dropped citation (Armstrong repository report), 1 wrong first author (Nunes, not Leporace), about 10 quoted values or details I could not find in the available text of the papers (McKean x4 values, Hemmerich ankle, bar dip, Van Gelder, Suprak and others, listed below), and a few small errors. Heavy was candid about its own UNSOURCED cells.
10. **Biggest practical flag:** published numbers are what people reached during the lift, not a minimum needed, and the app tests joints in isolation with a phone. So even the 15 SUPPORTS rows say "plausible", not "proven requirement". Ankle numbers are in degrees in the file but cm in the live app.

## How verdicts were decided (plain English)

- **VERIFIED** = the paper exists, the DOI/PMID matches title, author, year and journal, and I saw the quoted number (abstract, full-text table, or a full-text excerpt). **PARTIAL** = the paper exists but I could not see the number (full text not reachable) or something else did not match. **WRONG** = could not be confirmed as a paper.
- **SUPPORTS** = a published mean for that same joint and lift is at or above the app number, so the app number is a plausible requirement. **CONTRADICTS** = the app number is more than 20 deg above the highest published mean for that same lift (and above mean plus 1 SD), from a verified source. **PARTIAL/NOT COMPARABLE** = a paper exists but it measured a different thing (trunk lean vs lumbar, shoulder extension vs flexion, range vs angle, cm vs degrees), or the app number is above the published mean by 20 deg or less, or the source is capped (non-lifters, a borrowed lift, or unconfirmed numbers). **UNSOURCED** = no verified paper for that exercise and joint.
- Matching was done **exercise by exercise** (for example only the barbell hip thrust, not the glute bridge), then rolled up to the family. A family cell shows the verdict that most of its sourced numbers got; the table also says how many of its numbers had any comparator.

## Do the data support the beginner / intermediate / advanced ramp?

**No.** Honest answer in four points:

1. Almost every paper reports the angle people **reached** (a peak or mean) in one group, not a minimum, and not a comparison between skill or flexibility levels. I found no verified paper that splits these lifts into beginner, intermediate and advanced, or into low and high ROM groups.
2. The one study that does compare novices with experienced squatters (Lorenzetti 2018, 21 vs 21) reports frontal knee movement and joint moments in its abstract, not sagittal hip/knee/ankle ROM by level. So it is not a source for the tier numbers either (table values were not readable).
3. Where I can compare numerically, the pattern is: **beginner numbers are reached by published people (15 SUPPORTS, 0 CONTRADICTS), intermediate numbers are mostly above (4 CONTRADICTS vs 0 SUPPORTS) and advanced numbers are above (3 CONTRADICTS, 0 SUPPORTS).** The ramp steps up faster than anything the lifting literature shows people do. The tiers therefore look like a house rule (the earlier trace found the same value is stamped on every lift of a muscle group and tier), not a measured one.
4. Depth is a choice. Cotter 2013 shows the same people reach knee flexion of 99, 124 or 141 deg depending on the depth they are told to squat to. A tier ramp could be defined by prescribed depth, but then it is a coaching rule, not a ROM requirement.

**Citation errors found:**
- Nunes 2014 (J Med Imaging Health Inform): Heavy wrote first author "Leporace". Crossref lists de Oliveira Nunes first (Leporace is third). Title, DOI and journal are right. Status PARTIAL (no PubMed record; the 110.83 +/- 7.66 push-up figure and n=6 were not confirmed).
- Heavy's flattened tables ran PMIDs into the sample size (for example "PMID 17339814 national powerlifters" is PMID 1733981 plus "4 national powerlifters"). I corrected these; each corrected PMID resolves to the right paper.
- Step-up fluoroscopy paper had no author: it is Li 2013, Gait & Posture, with 21 healthy subjects (Heavy said "small cohort").
- Lorenzetti 2018 was cited with no DOI. It exists (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil, PMID 30026952). Not used as a number source.

**Numbers Heavy quoted that I could not find (paper exists, number unconfirmed):**
- McKean & Burkett 2015 (seated overhead press): shoulder flexion 135.9 / 147.3 / 144.7 / 155.0 deg. The abstract and every excerpt I could reach have no such values. Three app SUPPORTS verdicts lean on these, so treat them as unconfirmed.
- McKenzie 2022 bar dip 78.20 deg: not in the text (the paper gives 88.03% of the 88.72 deg maximum, which is about 78.1, so it is consistent).
- Hemmerich ankle 38.5 +/- 5.9 deg: not in Hemmerich's abstract. Schoenfeld 2010 (a review, secondary) is said to cite it; the primary ankle table appears to have n=26, not 30.
- Swinton 2012 "narrow stance 5-10 deg more ankle flexion": not in the abstract. A full-text excerpt shows start-of-concentric ankle flexion of 37.2 (traditional), 26.7 (powerlifting) and 14.4 deg (box), i.e. differences of 10.5 and 22.8 deg. Seen only via a search excerpt, so PARTIAL.
- Van Gelder 2015 n=19: abstract says 23 recruited; Suprak 2013 64.83 deg was labelled "shoulder elevation" but a secondary summary calls it humerothoracic abduction (the abstract says the instructed position was about 90 deg).
- Cotter 2013 numbers are mean +/- **standard error**, not SD as Heavy implied.
- Brazil 2021: Heavy said the absolute knee angle was not tabulated; it is (knee -108 +/- 8 deg at peak hip moment).
- Vigotsky 2015 hip range is 74.7-75.8, not 75.0-75.8.

**Cited papers that turned out not to measure angles (kept, but no numbers used):** Wretenberg 1996 and Escamilla 2001 leg press (no peak angles), Saeterbakken 2017 (EMG), van den Tillaar 2019, Kolber 2014, Muyor 2022, Marchetti 2015 and Schoenfeld 2013 reverse fly (EMG or other measures), Fry 2003, Schutz 2014, Straub & Powers 2024 and Swinton 2011 (no usable angle), Bryanton 2012 (its depth bins were not seen). Maeo 2021 is a hypertrophy study; its leg-curl hip angles are machine set-up angles, not measured performance.

## Main flags

1. **Peak, not minimum.** Published values are angles people reached during the lift. None says what is the least ROM needed to do the lift safely or well.
2. **Isolated test vs in-lift angle.** The app measures a joint in a standing or lying phone test. The angle definition may not equal the in-lift angle (anatomic 0 = straight vs relative angles). Examples: Lee 2018 ankle (111.9 deg conventional, 90.5 deg RDL) is an included-angle style, not clinical dorsiflexion; Vigotsky's ankle value is also an included plantar-flexion angle; Šarabon's Nordic knee angle is excursion from the start.
3. **Trunk flexion is not lumbar flexion.** All squat "lumbar flexion" support is trunk lean (35-41 deg at parallel). Only good morning (25-27.5 deg, n=15), bent-over row (22.9 deg, n=7) and hyperextension (31.1 deg, n=20) have a measured lumbar angle, and these are small lab studies.
4. **Ankle units.** The 48 ankle numbers (12 / 16 / 20) are stored as degrees in the file, but the live app measures knee-to-wall distance in CM. Published ankle dorsiflexion in squats and lunges ranges roughly 14-38 deg (Swinton, Kasahara, Comfort, Song). If the file numbers are degrees they are plausible; if they are meant as cm there is nothing to compare. All 7 ankle numbers that could be matched are marked PARTIAL/NOT COMPARABLE for this reason.
5. **Shoulder ER (86 numbers) and lumbar extension (29 numbers) have zero support.** No lift study measured them. All 26 biceps curl numbers are shoulder ER numbers, and I found no paper that treats shoulder rotation as a biceps curl requirement.
6. **Shoulder flexion is mostly not what the lifts measure.** Bench press studies report elbow flexion, shoulder abduction and horizontal adduction. Dips report shoulder extension (the opposite direction). Rows, pulldowns, raises, triceps lifts have no measured shoulder flexion at all.
7. **Hip flexion in non-squat lifts looks overstated.** Published: Nordic curl hip about 9 deg at peak torque, prone leg-curl machine hip set at about 30 deg (set-up, not measured), RDL about 80 deg, good morning about 75 deg, hip thrust about 83 deg (at peak hip moment), sumo about 87 deg (at the start of lift-off). The app asks 100-115 deg for the CONTRADICTS cases. Several are borderline (excess under 25 deg) and some comparators are single small studies.
8. **Mostly small samples.** Many studies have n=7-20, mostly young men. Female and older data are rare.
9. **Hemmerich 2006** (157 deg knee, 95 +/- 27 deg hip) is non-lifters doing a cultural deep squat, not a barbell squat.
10. **Coverage is thin.** Only 57 of 499 numbers match any paper; 442 would be unsupported if Jim insists on a peer-reviewed basis. See open decisions.

## Open decisions for Jim

1. **Keep the beginner/intermediate/advanced ramp as an explicit house rule?** The literature does not support it. If kept, label it a ROMRx progression standard, not "research-based".
2. **Grey out (or hide) the 442 unsourced numbers?** Or keep them visible but flagged "not yet sourced"? (Shoulder ER and lumbar extension would go entirely grey.)
3. **What to do with the 7 CONTRADICTS numbers** (Nordic curl, prone leg curl, sumo deadlift, two dumbbell RDL, good morning, hip thrust): lower them to published peaks, or keep as house stretch targets.
4. **Ankle unit:** decide whether the BB ankle numbers are degrees or cm before anything is compared or shown.
5. **Which standard replaces Perplexity?** Option: use healthy-adult joint norms (the JOINT-NORMS ankle/shoulder/hip rotation work) for the screening cutoffs, and use lift papers only as "typical peak in this lift" context.
6. **Commission a second pass** for the biggest unsourced families (crunch/sit-up 33, leg raise/core 33, biceps curl 26, pulldown 24, seated row 24, leg press 21, fly 20, face pull 19, calf raise 18): Heavy found no measured-angle papers, so these probably need new data, not another search.
7. **Confirm McKean 2015** numbers from the full text before any overhead-press shoulder flexion number is called supported.
